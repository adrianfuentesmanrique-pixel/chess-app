# Pulso — a live 1-v-1 puzzle tug-of-war between two friends

Spec written 2026-10-07. **No app code was written in this session.**
Status: waiting for Adrian's answers to the four decisions in section 2.
Until he answers, every number below uses the recommended option.

Plain-language summary: two friends get the same puzzles at the same moment. A
flame sits in the middle of a gold bar. Every puzzle you solve pulls the flame
toward your end, every mistake lets it slip back. Drag it all the way to your
end and you win on the spot; otherwise whoever holds more of the bar after three
minutes wins. Friends only. Both phones must be online with the app open.

## 2. Decisions Adrian needs to make

### D1. Match length
| Option | What it means |
|---|---|
| **A. 3 minutes (recommended)** | Same clock as the default Puzzle Rush. Long enough for the bar to swing twice, short enough to say "another one". |
| B. 2 minutes | Faster rematches, but the stronger player rarely gets pulled back. |
| C. 5 minutes | More comebacks, but one match costs about 70 % more of the daily free quota. |
| D. Challenger picks 3 or 5 | Flexible; one more control on the invite screen and twice the testing. |

### D2. What a mistake costs
| Option | What it means |
|---|---|
| **A. One step back, and your streak ends (recommended)** | A mistake exactly cancels a normal solve. Guessing never pays. |
| B. Two steps back | Very careful play; the bar moves slowly and a bad patch is hard to recover from. |
| C. No ground lost, streak ends only | Rewards speed and guessing — this is what makes it feel like Puzzle Racer. Not recommended. |

### D3. Do three strikes still end your run?
| Option | What it means |
|---|---|
| **A. No strikes in Pulso (recommended)** | A mistake already costs ground. Knocking a player out leaves the other pulling an empty rope for a minute — the opposite of a comeback. |
| B. Three strikes and you are frozen | Your board locks; the friend keeps pulling until the clock ends. |
| C. Three strikes and you lose at once | Simple, but most matches would end on mistakes rather than on the bar. |

### D4. Does a finished match show on the friend's profile?
| Option | What it means |
|---|---|
| **A. A private head-to-head tally (recommended)** | "Pulso: you 3 · Ana 2" on that friend's row and on their profile, visible **only to the two of you**. It lives inside the same match document, so it costs nothing extra. |
| B. Nothing is kept | The result screen is the only record. |
| C. Public on the profile | Anyone could see it. This is a leaderboard by the back door and you ruled leaderboards out. Not recommended. |

## 3. Rules of play

**The bar.** 21 places: the centre, and 10 steps to each side. Your end is
always drawn on the right of your own screen, the friend's on the left.

**The pull.**
- A solved puzzle pulls **1 step**.
- **Streak pull:** once you have solved 2 in a row, every further solve pulls
  **2 steps** until you make a mistake. The marker flame grows on your side
  while this is on. This is what makes a comeback possible.
- A mistake gives back **1 step**, ends your streak, and moves you to the next
  puzzle after the same 1.2-second pause Rush uses.
- No strikes. Nobody is ever knocked out.

**The marker's position** is never stored. Each player has a running "pull"
number; the marker sits at *my pull − your pull*, and both phones work it out
from the same two numbers.

**Winning.**
- Reach +10 (the marker at your end) → you win immediately.
- Otherwise the clock decides at **3:00**: whoever has the marker on their side.
- **Draw:** marker dead centre when time runs out → the player with **fewer
  mistakes** wins. Same mistakes too → it is a draw ("Tablas"), and the result
  screen offers a rematch.

**Three things that make it a struggle rather than a count:**
1. Mistakes give ground, so a lead can be thrown away.
2. The streak pull lets the player behind close a gap in a few puzzles.
3. The puzzles get harder by *position in the list*, not by score — so whoever
   is going faster is always working on harder puzzles than the one chasing.

**Estimated feel (not measured):** two equal players usually go to the clock;
a clearly stronger player wins by pulling the full bar in roughly 1½–2 minutes.

**Disconnects.** There is no server watching, so the rules are built so nothing
needs one:
- The end time is fixed when the match starts and both phones know it. The
  match always ends on the clock, even if one phone vanishes.
- Solves made while a phone is briefly offline are queued and count if they
  reach the server before the end time (plus 3 seconds of grace). After that
  they are refused.
- If the friend's number has not moved for 20 seconds, the match screen shows
  a small "no signal from Ana" note. Play carries on.
- **What reached the server is what counts.** The phone still connected at the
  end records the result.

**Leaving mid-match.**
- Tapping Leave (or the back button, or another tab) asks "Leave? You will lose
  this Pulso." Yes = you forfeit and the friend wins at once.
- Closing the app without answering is treated as a disconnect: your pull
  simply stops and the friend has the rest of the clock to drag the marker
  back. If you were far enough ahead you can still win — you earned that ground.

**Needs a connection.** Pulso is the one mode that cannot work offline. The
lobby says so and disables the challenge buttons while `navigator.onLine` is
false (the header already shows the offline icon).

**What Pulso does not touch:** puzzle ELO, badges, the leaderboards, Rush best
scores. It does count toward the daily streak if you solved 3 or more, using the
existing `STREAK_MIN_RUSH_SOLVED`.

## 4. Same puzzles for both phones

### The two ways

| | A. Challenger stores the list of puzzle ids | B. Both phones share a random seed |
|---|---|---|
| How | The challenger's phone picks 60 puzzles and writes their ids into the match document. | The document holds one number; each phone runs the same formula over its own puzzle files. |
| Identical? | **Yes, by construction** — both read the same list. | Only if both phones hold byte-identical puzzle files. |
| If the phones differ (one has not updated) | The friend's phone cannot find an id → clear message, match never starts. | **Nothing notices.** They play different puzzles and never find out. |
| Size in the document | 300 characters. | 1 number. |
| Download before a match | Both phones need the 6 puzzle files the match spans (about 3.1 MB, once; cached afterwards). | Each phone could fetch files as it goes. |
| Rules can check it | One pattern check on a 300-character string. | Trivial. |

### Recommendation: A, the stored list

A silent mismatch is the worst possible bug in a mode whose whole promise is
"same puzzles", and B cannot detect one. The cost of A is a one-time download,
and that download is also what makes the match fair: **both phones load every
puzzle before the clock starts**, so a slow connection can never stall one
player mid-match.

### The ramp (does not depend on anybody's score)

- 60 puzzles. Puzzle number *i* (0–59) has target rating
  `min(1950, 800 + 30 × i)` — it climbs for the first 39 puzzles and then stays
  at 1950.
- The challenger's phone picks puzzle *i* evenly from every puzzle within 50
  points of that target **and inside the same rating band**, widening the window
  exactly as `Rush.pickNext()` does if it holds too few, never repeating an id.
- The band is therefore known from *i* alone, which is how the friend's phone
  knows which file each id is in. Bands needed: 1 to 6 (800–1999), six files,
  3.13 MB on disk **[checked: file sizes added up]**. The cap is 1950 rather
  than 2000 precisely so a seventh file is never needed.
- Stored as one string, `pz`: the 60 five-character ids joined with nothing
  between them (300 characters). Every id in the library is exactly five
  letters/digits **[checked]**, so no separator is needed.
- Each player is on puzzle number *solved + mistakes*. Nobody waits for the
  other; the faster player is simply further down the same list.
- A player who finishes all 60 (20 a minute — unlikely) stops; their board shows
  "No puzzles left" and the clock decides.

### Before the clock starts

Both phones run `ensureBand()` for the needed bands and confirm that all 60 ids
are found. The challenger does this when the lobby opens; the friend does it
between tapping Accept and the accept being sent ("Preparing…"). If an id is
missing the friend's phone declines with "One of you needs to update the app".

## 5. Data model, rules and the free tier

### One document per pair of friends

`pulso/{pairId}` — `pairId` is the same sorted `uidA_uidB` a friendship uses.
One document per friendship, **reused for every match** between those two. That
gives "only one match at a time between two friends" and the head-to-head tally
for free.

"a" always means `members[0]`, "b" means `members[1]`.

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `members` | [uid, uid] sorted | Never changes after creation. |
| `status` | `'idle' \| 'invited' \| 'live' \| 'done'` | |
| `host` | uid | Who sent the current challenge. |
| `invitedAt` | server time | When the challenge was sent. It expires after 5 minutes. |
| `startAt` | server time or null | When the friend accepted. Play begins `startAt + 6 s`, ends `startAt + 186 s`. |
| `pz` | string, 300 chars | The 60 puzzle ids. |
| `aS` `aM` `aK` `aP` | whole numbers | Player a: solved, mistakes, current streak, pull. |
| `bS` `bM` `bK` `bP` | whole numbers | The same for player b. |
| `winner` | uid, `'draw'` or null | |
| `reason` | `'pull' \| 'time' \| 'left'` or null | |
| `aW` `bW` `dr` | whole numbers | Tally: a's wins, b's wins, draws. Only if D4 = A. |

### How the two clocks agree

Firestore has no "what time is it on the server" call. Each phone measures its
own offset from the one server-stamped write it makes anyway: the challenger
from the invite (`invitedAt`), the friend from the accept (`startAt`). Offset =
the stamped server time minus the midpoint of "sent" and "confirmed" on that
phone. Both then count down to `startAt + 6 s` in server time. The error is
about half of one network round trip — a fraction of a second.

### Who may write what (the rules, in words)

The rules are the referee. There is no Cloud Function.

1. **Read:** only if you are one of the two `members`.
2. **Create** (the first challenge ever between two friends): the id must match
   the sorted members (`pairIdOk`), you must be a member, **the friendship
   document must exist**, `status` must be `'invited'`, `host` must be you,
   `invitedAt == request.time`, `pz` must match `[A-Za-z0-9]{300}`, every counter
   and tally must be 0, `startAt`/`winner`/`reason` null, and no other fields.
3. **New challenge** (every later match, including a rematch): allowed for
   either member when the old status is `'idle'` or `'done'`, or `'invited'` and
   older than 5 minutes, or `'live'` and more than 10 seconds past its end
   (a match nobody closed). Same field checks as create, the friendship must
   still exist, and `members` and the tally must be unchanged.
4. **Cancel / decline:** the host may set an `'invited'` document to `'idle'`;
   so may the other member. Nothing else may change.
5. **Accept:** only the member who is **not** the host, only from `'invited'`,
   only inside the 5 minutes; sets `status: 'live'` and
   `startAt == request.time` and nothing else.
6. **A move** — the only write during play, and the heart of "each player may
   write only their own counters":
   - `status` is `'live'`, and `request.time` is between `startAt + 6 s` and
     `startAt + 189 s`;
   - the changed fields are a subset of **your own four** (`aS aM aK aP` if you
     are `members[0]`, the `b` set otherwise) — checked with `affectedKeys()`;
   - it is exactly one of:
     - a solve: `S + 1`, `K + 1`, `P + 1` if the old `K` was under 2, else `P + 2`, `M` unchanged;
     - a mistake: `M + 1`, `K = 0`, `P − 1`, `S` unchanged;
   - `S + M` never exceeds 60.
7. **Ending the match:** either member, only from `'live'`, sets
   `status: 'done'` with `winner` and `reason`, and the rule checks the claim
   against the numbers already stored:
   - `'pull'`: `aP − bP ≥ 10` and the winner is a, or `≤ −10` and it is b;
   - `'time'`: `request.time ≥ startAt + 189 s` (the end of play plus the 3
     seconds of grace, so a last solve still in the air is counted before the
     result is frozen), and the winner is whoever is
     ahead; level → fewer mistakes; still level → `'draw'`;
   - `'left'`: the winner is **the other** player (you can only forfeit yourself).
   The matching tally field goes up by exactly 1; no counter may change.
8. **Delete:** never (a friend blocking you just leaves a document neither of
   you opens again).

A phone sends the "ending" write only after it sees the winning numbers in a
snapshot the **server has confirmed**, not in its own optimistic copy. If both
phones send it, the second is refused because the status is no longer `'live'`,
and the app ignores that refusal. If the friend pulled back in the instant
before, the claim is refused and play simply continues — the tie goes to the
defender.

**Friendship is checked on a challenge, not on every move.** A rule lookup of
another document is billed as a read, so it is done once per match. Reading
needs only membership. Honest consequence: if two people unfriend, each can
still read the old document (puzzle ids and old counters, nothing personal) but
neither can start a match.

### One listener for everything

A new `watchPulso(cb)` in `js/firebase.js`, built like `watchLiveState()`:
`onSnapshot` on `pulso` where `members array-contains` my uid (no composite index
needed — the same shape as the friends query), with
`includeMetadataChanges: true`. It runs while a signed-in user has the app open
and delivers both incoming challenges and the live match. It would be the
second listener in the app, and the first one that is always on.

### Cost of one match against the free tier

Free tier per day: **50,000 reads, 20,000 writes** [checked].

| Step | Writes | Reads |
|---|---|---|
| Challenge sent | 1 | 2 (one to each phone) + 1 for the friendship check |
| Accepted | 1 | 2 |
| Each puzzle attempt, both players | 1 each | 2 each (assumed worst case: both phones billed, including the writer's own echo) |
| Result recorded | 1 | 2 |

| Match | Attempts (both players) | Writes | Reads |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical (about 22 each) | 44 | **47** | **about 95** |
| Hard ceiling (60 each) | 120 | **123** | **about 247** |

Plus, outside matches: opening the app costs **1 read per friend you have ever
played Pulso with** (the listener's first load).

What that buys per day, if Pulso were the only thing writing: about **425
typical matches**, or 160 at the ceiling. **Writes run out long before reads.**
Homework, sync and Masterclass share the same 20,000, and when the quota is gone
every save in the whole app fails until midnight Pacific. At friends-and-family
scale this is far away; if Pulso ever gets popular, the fix is to send a
player's moves in small bundles (the rule in point 6 would allow "+n" instead
of "+1"). Not built now.

## 6. How a friend finds out they were challenged

**Honestly:** they only find out if their app is open. There are no push
notifications (parked 2026-08-20), so a closed app hears nothing.

So the design does two things:

1. **In the app:** the always-on listener shows a gold banner at the top of
   whatever screen the friend is on: "Ana te reta a un Pulso — Aceptar · Ahora
   no". It is a banner, not a pop-up, and it **never interrupts** a running Rush,
   a game against the engine or a live Masterclass — there it shrinks to a gold
   dot on the Puzzles tab until they finish. It disappears when the challenge
   expires (5 minutes) or is cancelled.
2. **Outside the app:** the challenger's waiting screen says plainly "Ana has to
   have the app open" and has a **"Tell them" button** that opens the phone's
   share sheet (`navigator.share`, text only) with: "Te reto a un Pulso en Chess
   Training Center. Abre la app — tienes 5 minutos." No link tricks, no
   notification: the friend opens the app, the listener loads, the banner is
   there.

Where a challenge starts: a fourth button, **Pulso**, on the puzzle-mode strip
(next to Puzzles, Blindfold, Rush) opens the Pulso lobby — the friends list with
a Challenge button and the tally on each row. A "Challenge to a Pulso" item is
also added to the top of the ⋯ menu on a row in Friends, and a button on a
friend's public profile.

## 9. Cheating, said plainly

Everything is decided on the players' own phones, and every puzzle's solution
is already in the files the app downloads. So a friend who edits the app, or
types into the browser console, **can**:

- **Claim solves they did not make.** One at a time, up to 60, each pulling 1 or
  2 steps — enough to win any match in seconds.
- **Look up the answers**, or let an engine play.
- **Read the 60 puzzle ids** the moment the challenge arrives and prepare.
- **Dodge a loss** by closing the app (though the result is still recorded by
  the other phone when the clock runs out).

What they **cannot** do, because the rules refuse it:

- change the friend's counters, or lower them;
- write a result the stored numbers do not support, or name themselves the
  winner of a forfeit;
- add wins to the tally without winning a match the rules accepted;
- keep scoring after the end time;
- start or read a match with someone who is not their confirmed friend, or read
  anybody else's match.

**Why that is acceptable:** a Pulso result earns nothing — no rating, no badge,
no leaderboard place, nothing a stranger can see. The only thing a cheat can win
is a number shown to the one friend they cheated, who will notice 60 solves in
ten seconds. Stopping it properly needs a server that replays every move, which
means Cloud Functions and the paid plan. If Pulso ever gets a public ranking,
this section stops being true and the design must be reopened.

## 10. Build plan — one session each

Each session ends with the checks named, a commit, and the next prompt.

**Session 1 — Rules and tests (no screens).**
The `pulso/{pairId}` block in `firestore.rules`, written from section 5, and
`tests/rules/pulso.test.js` with one test per clause, including: a stranger
cannot create; a non-friend cannot create; a player cannot change the friend's
counters; +2 without a streak is refused; a move after the end time is refused;
a false `'pull'` claim is refused; a second "done" is refused; the tally cannot
be changed except by a valid result. `npm.cmd run test:rules` green.
**Not deployed** — `npm.cmd run rules:deploy` is a separate step Adrian approves.

**Session 2 — Make Rush's engine reusable; no visible change.**
In `Rush`: every `$('rush-…')` goes through one small lookup using a prefix, and
the three places that are Rush-only (what a solve does, what a mistake does,
where the next puzzle comes from) become overridable methods. Rush must behave
exactly as before — re-run the 40-runs-by-10-puzzles measurement from the v159
entry in `HANDOVER.md`, and play one run by hand at 375 px.

**Session 3 — The sequence and the data layer.**
New `js/pulso.js` (added to `ASSETS` in `sw.js`): the pure functions — target
rating by index, building the 60-id list, finding a puzzle by id, the pull
arithmetic, deciding the result from the counters. Unit tests in `tests/unit/`.
In `js/firebase.js`: `watchPulso`, `sendPulsoChallenge`, `cancelPulso`,
`acceptPulso`, `pulsoMove`, `finishPulso`, and the clock-offset measurement.
Checked against the emulator, no screens yet.

**Session 4 — Lobby, challenge, waiting, banner.**
`screen-pulso` added to `SCREENS` and `index.html`; the Pulso button on all
three copies of the puzzle-mode strip; lobby, waiting screen, "Tell them",
incoming banner, cancel, decline, expiry. Two browser profiles signed in as two
friends. 375 px, light and dark, ES and EN.

**Session 5 — The match.**
`Pulso` built on the Rush engine from session 2: countdown to the shared start,
the bar, the flame, streak pull, the friend's moves arriving, no-signal note,
leave/forfeit, the end write, `showScreen` stopping Pulso like it stops Rush.
Two profiles play a full match; one is cut off mid-match to prove the clock
still ends it.

**Session 6 — Result, rematch, tally, profile.**
Result screen for all seven endings, rematch from both sides, the tally on the
lobby row and (per D4) on the friend's public profile, share card, daily-streak
credit.

**Session 7 — Art and release.**
Adrian's files into `icons/pulso/`, `ART`, `ART_RE`, `ART_DIRS`,
`npm.cmd run test:precache`; bump `CACHE`; rules deploy and push to `main`
**only when Adrian says so**; then a real match on two phones.

Sessions 1 and 2 do not depend on each other and can be done in either order.
Session 7's art half waits on Adrian's drawings; everything else works with the
stand-in flames.

## 11. Deliberately left out

Public matchmaking, recorded "shadow" opponents, leaderboards, a weekly cup,
notifications, more than two players, spectators, chat or emotes, choosing a
puzzle theme, and playing each puzzle as a first-to-solve round (considered and
dropped: it needs both players writing shared fields, and the slower player
spends the match having puzzles taken away mid-thought).
